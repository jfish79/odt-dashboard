About this database

This database tracks same-day, app- or phone-bookable public on-demand transit deployments across the United States — commonly called microtransit or demand-response transit. It is a research inventory, not an official government registry. Data is compiled from agency websites, press releases, state DOT studies, and the Federal Transit Administration. All coverage areas and fleet figures are approximate.

Field definitions
Geographic Context
Characterizes the land-use environment of the primary service area. Assigned by the research team based on Census urbanized area classification and service zone description.
Urban
Service area is primarily within a city core or densely developed urban district. Typically characterized by Census-defined urbanized areas with >50,000 population, walkable street grids, and connections to rail or frequent bus.
Examples: GoGastonia (NC), Via Jersey City (NJ), MARTA Reach (GA), Metro Micro zones (CA)
Suburban
Service area is primarily within a suburban municipality or outer metropolitan ring. Typically lower density than urban, auto-oriented land use, often first/last-mile to commuter rail or park-and-ride. May include Census "urban cluster" areas (2,500–50,000 population).
Examples: RTD FlexRide zones (CO), Metro Flex (WA), Ride Gwinnett (GA), CATS Micro (NC)
Rural
Service area is primarily in a rural county or low-density region outside any Census urbanized area. Trips are typically longer, vehicles are fewer, and connections to fixed-route transit are limited or absent. Often demand-response in nature.
Examples: YVEDDI GOTransit (NC), Clallam Transit Interlink (WA), Bay Transit Express (VA), GoWake SmartRide NE (NC)
Mixed
Service area meaningfully crosses multiple context types — for example, a military installation with adjacent civilian communities, or a zone bridging a city core and outlying suburban areas.
Examples: Cavazos Connector (TX — military/urban), The HOP (TX — multi-city urban/rural)
System Type
Distinguishes the scheduling model of the service.
Microtransit
Rides are booked same-day, with no fixed schedule or route. The algorithm dynamically routes vehicles in real time to serve multiple passengers. Pickup is typically within 15–30 minutes of request. Synonymous with "on-demand transit" in this database.
Demand-Response
Rides are typically booked ahead of time, but same-day booking must still be a genuine, routinely-honored option — a system requiring booking by some cutoff on a day before the trip, with no same-day path, is out of scope regardless of the exact hour count. Vehicles are dynamically routed but scheduled rather than immediately dispatched. Common in rural settings. Open to the general public. (Scope change 2026-08-27: previously any "Advance" scheduling window qualified; a full sweep removed ~215 rows dataset-wide that had no working same-day option.)
Status / Maturity
Describes the operational lifecycle stage of the service.
ActiveCurrently operating with stable funding and no announced end date.
Active – ExpandingActively operating and adding zones, fleet, or service hours.
Active – NewRecently launched (approximately within the last 6 months of the database's last update).
Active – Post PilotGraduated from a time-limited pilot into permanent or ongoing funded service.
PilotActively operating under a time-limited or experimental funding authorization.
Pilot – EndedPilot has concluded. Permanent status (continuation, suspension, or transition) unknown or pending announcement.
Scheduling Window
How far in advance rides must be booked.
Same-dayRides can be booked and received within the same operating day, typically within minutes to an hour of request. The defining characteristic of true on-demand / microtransit.
AdvanceAdvance booking (typically a day ahead) is the norm, but a same-day request is still a genuine option. Common in rural demand-response programs. Systems with no same-day path at all are out of scope (see Scope rules).
FlexibleSystem supports both same-day and advance booking, often with different lead times for different trip types or zones.
Technology Vendor
The software platform provider powering the dispatch, routing, and rider app. Distinct from the operator (who runs the vehicles) and the funding agency (who pays for the service). Many agencies procure the transit management contract and the technology platform separately.
ViaNew York-based TransitTech company, the most common platform in this database. Powers both app and operations management. Agencies often brand the service independently of Via's name.
SpareVancouver-based platform focused on transit agency integration, paratransit, and multi-modal trip planning. Used by DART GoLink, CATS Micro, Metro Transit Micro, and others.
RideCoToronto-based on-demand transit software. Used by LA Metro Micro, Ride Gwinnett, OmniRide (Omnitrans), and others.
FreebeeMiami-based operator-and-software provider. Unlike pure software vendors, Freebee also operates the vehicles. Predominantly deployed in Florida municipalities.
The Routing Company (TRC/Pingo)Boston-based platform. Consumer app is branded "Pingo." Used by Clallam Transit Interlink, CDTA FLEX+, FAST Connect.
Not specifiedThe technology vendor is unknown, the agency uses a proprietary system, or the agency has not publicly disclosed their software provider.
Service area boundaries

The Map view draws a service-area polygon for most systems when a state is selected. Almost no on-demand transit agency publishes downloadable boundary data, so polygons come from four sources, in order of preference:

  1. The agency's own geofence from a GTFS-Flex feed (locations.geojson), where one exists. A September 2026 scan of the Mobility Database catalog and of Trillium Transit's hosted feeds found usable geofences for a handful of systems; five other name-matched feeds were rejected because their geofence belonged to a different route on the same feed (typically ADA paratransit).
  2. U.S. Census Bureau TIGER/Line cartographic boundary files (2023 vintage, 1:500k): incorporated places, county subdivisions (New England towns, townships), counties, and American Indian / Alaska Native / Native Hawaiian areas, chosen to match the service area the agency describes.
  3. Local neighborhood geographies for a few North Carolina zones: Charlotte Neighborhood Profile Areas and Census tracts geocoded from the boundary streets the agency lists.
  4. Stated-size circles where the agency gives only a zone size ("about 12 square miles"), centered on the zone's town and clipped to it.

North Carolina was digitized by hand in June 2026; the other 49 states were drafted from each row's service-area text by an agent-assisted pipeline in September 2026, audited, and web-researched where flagged. Every polygon's popup lists what it was built from and whether it is exact or approximate. The choices are recorded per system in data/boundaries/specs/, so the GeoJSON can be regenerated deterministically. Six multi-zone systems have no polygon yet because their zones are named only by neighborhood (GRTC LINK, MARTA Reach, Metro Micro, Mid-City GO, Southeastern Connect, FAST Connect).

Data caveats

Fleet figures are sourced from press releases and agency reports and may not reflect current operations. Service hours and fares change frequently — verify with the agency before travel planning. Latitude/longitude pins represent approximate service area centers; where a state has boundary overlays, the polygon shows the documented coverage area at the fidelity noted in its popup. Systems marked "Not specified" have data that is not publicly available or not yet researched. The filters and analytics use normalized categories derived at build time (vendor names, status, geographic context, launch year); the full researcher text is shown on each card and in the detail view.

Data quality concerns

Notable data-quality issues found while integrating supplemental lists (June 2026 added 120 CA/CO systems) and during later state-by-state sweeps are summarized below. The full running log, with one entry per system and decision, is concerns.csv in the project repository.

Vendor errors (verified)
Five systems in the supplemental lists had incorrect technology vendors. All were independently verified against vendor press releases and agency sources. The existing database values are correct; the supplemental values were discarded during integration.

Paratransit scope change (2026-07)
83 systems from the supplemental CA/CO lists were originally reclassified from "Demand-Response" to a separate "Paratransit" System Type, based on eligibility restrictions found in their names or notes (ADA, senior, disabled, age cutoffs). As of July 2026, paratransit was removed from scope entirely rather than tracked as a distinct type — all 170 eligibility-restricted rows database-wide (not just the 83 from the CA/CO integration) were removed, and "Paratransit" was retired as a System Type. This database now covers general-public on-demand transit only; eligibility-certified-rider-only services are out of scope regardless of how demand-responsive they are. Each original reclassification (and the later removal) is logged in concerns.csv.
24-hour-advance scope change (2026-08)
Demand-Response rows were originally included whenever they carried an "Advance" scheduling window and were open to the general public. As of August 2026, the rule was clarified: same-day booking must be a genuine, routinely-honored option (advance can be preferred or the norm, but a rider must actually be able to book and ride same-day, even with a short lead time like a 2-hour notice). A full dataset sweep checked every Advance-family row against its primary source and removed ~215 that required booking by some cutoff on a day before the trip with no same-day path, regardless of whether that cutoff was technically under 24 hours. Roughly 40 more were corrected in place once their source showed same-day genuinely was accepted. A residual set of rows remain logged as unconfirmed (no source stated a policy either way) and were left in scope rather than removed on a guess — each is in concerns.csv as a Data Gap entry needing a phone call. Full per-system citations are there, dated 2026-08-27.
Status change: OC Flex
OC Flex (Orange County, CA) was discontinued effective July 1, 2025 per OCTA's website. The supplemental list had status "Active"; the row was set to "Pilot – Ended" in September 2026 and is retained for the record.
Deduplication
Two pre-existing duplicate pairs were resolved: CATS Micro (NC) had two entries with 8 differing fields — the more detailed entry was kept. Cupertino Microtransit and SV Hopper (CA) were the same Via-operated service listed twice — the richer SV Hopper entry was kept. Additionally, 12 systems that appeared in both the existing database and the supplemental lists were skipped (existing entries retained, since supplemental vendors were unreliable).
Coordinate precision
Coordinates for supplemental CA/CO entries typically point to a city hall or downtown centroid rather than the service zone center. Adequate for the national map view but imprecise for zoomed views.
